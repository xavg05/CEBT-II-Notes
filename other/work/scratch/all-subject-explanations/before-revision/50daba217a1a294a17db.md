# Diffusion in porous catalysts

## Overview

Assume Fick's law and molecular kinetic theory. Effective diffusion accounts for accessible pore fraction, winding paths and the physical collisions that restrict transport. State the flux area and concentration basis before using a diffusivity.

## Intuition

Small pores can make collisions with walls more frequent than collisions with other gas molecules. Strongly bound molecules may move mainly by hopping along the surface. Neither situation behaves like unconstrained bulk-gas diffusion.

## Derivation and models

Define effective diffusivity $D_e$ through macroscopic flux $J=-D_e\nabla C$, where $J$ is per total pellet cross-sectional area and $C$ is pore-fluid concentration. A common empirical model is

$$D_e=\frac{\varepsilon_p}{\tau_D}D_{\mathrm{pore}},\qquad
\varepsilon_p=\frac{\text{pore volume}}{\text{total pellet volume}}.$$

Here $\tau_D$ is a diffusion tortuosity factor including the convention used for winding, connectivity and constriction. A geometric path-length ratio and an effective diffusion factor are not necessarily identical; some conventions use the square of the path-length ratio. Quote or fit the factor consistently rather than inserting a geometrical ratio blindly. Typical pellet porosities and empirical factors depend on preparation and cannot be assumed universal.

For molecular diffusion, kinetic theory estimates $D_m\sim\lambda\bar c/3$, where $\lambda$ is molecular mean free path and $\bar c$ mean thermal speed. For molecule mass $m$, Boltzmann constant $k_B$ and gas temperature $T$,

$$\bar c=\sqrt{\frac{8k_BT}{\pi m}}=\sqrt{\frac{8\mathcal RT}{\pi M}},$$

where $M$ is molar mass in $\mathrm{kg\,mol^{-1}}$. A dilute hard-sphere estimate $\lambda\sim k_BT/(\sqrt2P\sigma_c)$ uses collision cross-section $\sigma_c$. Thus gas molecular diffusivity generally rises with temperature and falls with pressure. Multicomponent transport often requires binary coefficients or a more complete Maxwell–Stefan treatment.

Knudsen diffusion dominates when mean free path exceeds pore width, as in small pores or at low pressure. For a cylindrical pore with diameter $d_{\mathrm{pore}}$, the wall-collision estimate is

$$D_K=\frac{d_{\mathrm{pore}}}{3}\sqrt{\frac{8\mathcal RT}{\pi M}}.$$

It varies as pore diameter and $T^{1/2}$, with weak direct pressure dependence. In a simple dilute gas model with both molecular and wall collisions, their collision frequencies add, giving the Bosanquet combination

$$\frac1{D_{\mathrm{gas,pore}}}\approx\frac1{D_m}+\frac1{D_K}.$$

The smaller diffusivity limits transport. Molecular and Knudsen transport are not independent parallel channels whose diffusivities should simply be added. The reciprocal form is an approximation; multicomponent diffusion, viscous flow and very narrow pores need fuller models.

Adsorbed molecules can diffuse along pore walls by activated hopping. A simple surface coefficient is $D_s=D_{s0}\exp[-E_{\mathrm{diff}}/(\mathcal RT)]$, with hopping barrier $E_{\mathrm{diff}}$. In molecular-width pores, constrained or configurational transport may dominate even without strongly attractive sites.

If gas and adsorbed phases offer parallel transport, their **fluxes** add. Write $J_s=-D_s a_v\,d\Gamma/dx$, where $\Gamma(C)$ is adsorbed amount per wall area and $a_v$ wall area per pellet volume. Combined flux becomes

$$J=-\left[D_{e,g}+D_s a_v\frac{d\Gamma}{dC}\right]\frac{dC}{dx}.$$

Only after converting to this common area and concentration basis can a surface contribution be added to an effective gas diffusivity. The term $a_vd\Gamma/dC$ supplies the needed units and loading dependence.

## Motivation and explanation

Measurements probing one pore, an entire pellet and a packed bed report different transport processes and length scales. A bed-scale dispersion coefficient is not a pellet diffusion coefficient. Strong adsorption can change both the available fluid concentration and the surface transport contribution; it also weakens the assumption that diffusion has little temperature dependence.

## Worked example

For illustrative $D_m=10^{-5}$ and $D_K=2\times10^{-6}\ \mathrm{m^2\,s^{-1}}$, Python gives $D_{\mathrm{gas,pore}}=1.66667\times10^{-6}\ \mathrm{m^2\,s^{-1}}$. With $\varepsilon_p=0.4$ and $\tau_D=3$, the effective gas coefficient is $2.22222\times10^{-7}\ \mathrm{m^2\,s^{-1}}$. A direct sum would give $1.2\times10^{-5}$ before porosity correction, incorrectly predicting easier transport when an additional collision mechanism restricts it.

## Link to other topics

The effective coefficient enters pellet diffusion–reaction balances. Adsorption measurements and pore-size information help identify whether gas, wall or surface transport is relevant.

## Conclusion

Combine mechanisms on a consistent flux basis. Distinguish molecular-plus-wall collision resistance from genuinely parallel gas and surface pathways.
