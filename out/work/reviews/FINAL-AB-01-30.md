## Corrections requiring integration

- AB-09: genome transplantation uses an intact recipient; its original chromosome is subsequently lost, rather than removed beforehand.
- AB-11: end repair removes protruding 3′ ends with 3′→5′ exonuclease activity and fills recessed 3′ ends opposite 5′ overhangs.
- AB-13–14: the consensus example numbers did not consistently match the same-wrong-base majority model, which also omits plurality errors and ties. Replace them with an explicit conservative independent-binomial upper bound, counting ties as failures. At p=0.05, 5/10/30 reads give bounds 0.0011581/0.0000636898/2.3062e-12. At p=0.12, 1/3/5/10 passes give bounds 0.12/0.039744/0.0143189/0.00371607. State clearly that these are illustrative statistical bounds, not empirical sequencing quality or a guarantee that six to seven passes produce HiFi reads. Remove categorical independence and all-reads-identically-wrong claims.
- AB-15: distinguish a 50-base sequencing read from the approximately 150–200 bp cell-free DNA fragment distribution (prominent nucleosomal peak near 166 bp).
- AB-17: derive ΔCt with distinct target and reference assay intercepts. Their difference remains after the first subtraction and cancels after the second; ΔCt alone does not yield an absolute cross-assay abundance ratio. Correct the required assay consistency condition. Routine melting analysis cannot prove sequence identity, and heterozygotes need not produce two resolved peaks.
- AB-18: distinguish 5′ nuclease probe cleavage from 3′→5′ proofreading. Most, rather than every, eukaryotic mRNA carries a poly-A tail. SARS-CoV-2 genomic RNA is polyadenylated; virus-specific reverse-transcription primers are a design choice.
- AB-21: a filled 100 nm square at 2.5 nm helix spacing would require about 11,765 bp, exceeding a 7249-nt scaffold. The same filled-square approximation instead gives a side near 78.5 nm.
- AB-22: the short-toehold limit is kBM/rho0 × exp(−ΔG°/RT), not kBM×rho0 × exp(−ΔG°/RT). Its units are M⁻¹ s⁻¹ as required.
- AB-23: alpha-helical hydrogen bonds connect CO at i to NH at i+4, not the opposite direction.
- AB-28: log-linear time decay differs from dose-proportional pharmacokinetics. Saturating a target-mediated elimination pathway at high concentrations can cause nonspecific first-order clearance to dominate; the original concentration-dependence claim was reversed.
